FOOD FOR THOUGHT

MASID-DANAS

In Happyland, a community in Barangay 105, Tondo, Manila, many residents face poverty and harsh living conditions, as described by Segarra (2023). The area is characterized by overcrowded shantytowns and unstable shacks, surrounded by waste and unpleasant odors. Due to financial difficulties and a lack of affordable food, some residents turn to pagpag, which consists of discarded food, particularly leftover meat and bones, that is collected, cleaned, and cooked again. EFE observed collectors like Roweno Cabuluc gathering discarded food from restaurants and fast-food chains and salvaging pieces of meat that still appear edible, which are then cleaned and prepared by vendors such as Evelyn Blasorca for sale. The affordability, accessibility, portion size, familiarity, and taste of pagpag make it a common food choice among consumers.

A study by Alonzo et al. (2024) involving 387 adult pagpag consumers from Happyland, Aroma, and Payatas-B revealed that motivations for purchasing pagpag included portion size compared with ordinary eateries (3.95), familiarity (3.95), and budget (3.91), with an overall weighted mean (average survey score) of 3.61, interpreted as Strongly Agree. Taste (3.91) and hunger relief (3.86) were also strong motivations for consumption, both interpreted as Strongly Agree.

However, reliance on pagpag raises health concerns, as repeated consumption may contribute to malnutrition and foodborne illnesses, particularly among children (Philippine National Anti-Poverty Commission, as cited in Segarra, 2023). Respondents also showed limited awareness of these potential health risks, with overall weighted means of 3.07 and 3.22 for immediate symptoms and diseases, respectively, both interpreted as Not Aware (Alonzo et al., 2024).

In response to food insecurity, Project PEARLS operates a Hunger Relief Program that reports having provided over 230,000 nutritious meals across its supported communities since 2010, aiming to combat hunger and support children's education (Project PEARLS, n.d.). These efforts contribute to Sustainable Development Goal 2: Zero Hunger, which aims to ensure access to sufficient, safe, and nutritious food.
